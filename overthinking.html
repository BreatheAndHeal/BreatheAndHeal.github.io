OVERTHINKING

Why Do I Keep Overthinking?

When the same thought keeps returning, the goal is not to fight your mind harder. It is to create enough distance to notice what the thought is doing.

First, separate the event from the story

Write down what actually happened in one sentence. Then write what your mind is predicting, assuming or replaying around it. Those are different things.

Overthinking often feels like problem-solving because it is full of activity. A useful question is: “Is this thought helping me decide something, or is it asking me to replay it again?”

Give the loop a smaller job

Instead of trying to solve your entire future, choose one question that can be answered today. The smaller the question, the easier it is to act instead of circle around it.

A short walk, a page of writing, or talking to someone you trust can sometimes interrupt a loop long enough to see the next useful step.

When a conversation can help

You do not need to arrive with a perfect explanation. Sometimes saying the whole thought out loud reveals what is actually underneath it: fear, uncertainty, grief, guilt or a decision you have been avoiding.

A note from The Unsaid: This is reflective, general information, not a diagnosis or a substitute for professional care. When something feels overwhelming, talking to a qualified professional or someone you trust may be the right next step.
